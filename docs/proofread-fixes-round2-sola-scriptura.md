## C. `ai-quyet-dinh-sach-nao-thuoc-kinh-thanh` — reframe + consistency · Session 3

**C1 — Reframe the objection (most important fix in this batch).** The site's method is *state the
objection fairly, then answer it* — but this Q&A states a **Catholic talking point** as if it were the
Protestant's objection. A Protestant wouldn't ask "who decided?"; they'd say **the Church did not
*authorize* the books, it merely *recognized* what was already inspired.** Restate it that way, then
answer: recognition is still an act of *judgment* — someone had to discern which books bore the marks
of inspiration, and that discerning body is the Church. (Owner's own framing; it's the honest one.)

**C2 — Standardize the canon vocabulary.** The Q&A uses **"danh sách sách thánh"**, then **"Danh mục"**
(quoting the CCC), then **"bảng mục lục"** for the same concept. Pick one and use it throughout — **match
the Catechism's term** (the in-house authority) and gloss the others once if they must appear.

**C3 — "và được ít người coi như Kinh Thánh"** — odd phrasing; reword (intended sense: "regarded by only
a few as Scripture").

**C4 — Rephrase the clunky sentence:** *"Không thể nhận quyền của Hội Thánh để có được bảng mục lục Kinh
Thánh, rồi lại phủ nhận rằng Hội Thánh có quyền dạy ý nghĩa của chính những sách mình đã công nhận."*
Split into two sentences; the argument is good, the sentence is overloaded.

## E. ⚠️ VERIFY BEFORE USING — two claims that could be rebutted · Session 3
Per `CLAUDE.md` "Verify facts before they ship."

**E1 — *paradosis* translated "teaching" instead of "tradition." ✅ VERIFIED 2026-08-20 (coordinator).
The claim is HALF true — do NOT write it as stated.**

**CGKPV (Catholic) uses the plain word in both verses:**
- **2 Th 2,15** — "nắm giữ **các truyền thống** chúng tôi đã dạy cho anh em"
- **1 Cr 11,2** — "nắm giữ **các truyền thống** tôi đã để lại cho anh em"

**Vietnamese Protestant versions are SPLIT** (1 Cr 11,2, verified via HTTLVN's own comparison tool):

| Version | Renders *paradosis* as |
|---|---|
| **VI1934 / Bản Truyền Thống** (oldest, most used) | "những điều **dạy dỗ**" — *teachings* |
| **BPT** (Bản Phổ Thông) | "những điều tôi **khuyên dạy**" — *instructions* |
| **RVV11** (HTTLVN's own 2011 revision) | "**các truyền thống**" — *traditions* |
| **BD2011** | "những **truyền thống**" — *traditions* |

2 Th 2,15 in VI1934 likewise reads "vâng giữ **những điều dạy dỗ**".

⚠️ **So "Protestant Bibles hide the word" is false as a general claim** — RVV11 and BD2011 say
*truyền thống* exactly as CGKPV does. Writing it as stated would be **inaccurate and uncharitable**,
against the content-guide's charitable-tone rule, and trivially rebutted by any reader holding an RVV11.

**Better argument — and it doesn't depend on translation at all.** Scripture uses **the same Greek word
*paradosis*** for the tradition Paul tells us to *hold fast* (2 Th 2,15; 1 Cr 11,2) and for the "truyền
thống của loài người" Jesus *condemns* (Mc 7,8-13). So the Bible itself doesn't reject tradition as
such — it distinguishes **apostolic** tradition from merely **human** tradition. That is the real point,
it stands on the Greek rather than on any translator's choice, and it can't be rebutted by producing a
different Vietnamese Bible.

*(If a translation note is still wanted, phrase it narrowly: "the older Bản Truyền Thống renders it
'điều dạy dỗ'; newer versions such as RVV11 use 'truyền thống'" — factual, no accusation.)*

**E2 — the *apostellō* argument. ✅ VERIFIED 2026-08-20 (coordinator) — the argument WORKS, but move it
to a different verse.**

The flag was correct: **Ga 20,21 does switch verbs** — ἀπέσταλκέν (*apostellō*, perfect) for the Father
sending Christ, but **πέμπω** (*pempō*, present) for Christ sending the disciples. Pinning the argument
on *apostellō* there is rebuttable.

**Use Ga 17,18 instead — it uses *apostellō* BOTH times:**
> *καθὼς ἐμὲ **ἀπέστειλας** εἰς τὸν κόσμον, κἀγὼ **ἀπέστειλα** αὐτοὺς εἰς τὸν κόσμον*
> "Như Cha đã sai con đến thế gian, thì con cũng sai họ đến thế gian." (Ga 17,18)

Same verb, Father→Son and Son→disciples. That is precisely the point the owner wants, with nothing to
rebut. **Keep Ga 20,21-23 as well** — it still carries the sending + the Spirit + the authority to
forgive sins, and one commentary tradition reads **17,18 as proleptic, fulfilled in 20,21**, which lets
both verses work together rather than forcing a choice.

⚠️ **Do NOT build the argument on an *apostellō* vs *pempō* contrast.** Whether the distinction is
significant is **genuinely contested**: some hold *apostellō* = commission with delegated authority vs
*pempō* = general sending, others hold that John alternates them as stylistic variation (characteristic
of his Greek). Per `CLAUDE.md`, present contested scholarship as contested — or simply avoid needing it,
which 17,18 lets us do.

**Facts confirmed:** *apostellō* (Strong's 649) occurs **~133×** in the NT — Mt 10,5 and Mc 3,14
(sending the Twelve), Lc 10,1 (the seventy), Ga 3,17 and 5,36 (the Father sending the Son), Cv 13,4.
*apostolos* does derive from *apostellō*. Sources: BibleHub interlinears for Ga 17,18 and Ga 20,21 +
Strong's 649 lexicon entry.

📌 **Session 3:** quote the Vietnamese from **CGKPV** as always, and verify the Ga 17,18 wording in the
CGKPV file rather than translating the Greek yourself.

## F. New Q&A — "even the Church Fathers disagreed" · Session 3
Proposed by the owner. Protestant objection: *"the Fathers didn't agree on the canon, so how do we know
the Catholic Church got it right?"* Three answers:
1. **It cuts both ways** — Protestants use essentially the same NT canon, so the objection undermines
   every Christian's Bible equally, not just the Catholic one.
2. **Infallibility was never promised to every individual** — Christ promised Peter and the Church as a
   whole, not that no Father would ever err.
3. **(add this)** The disputes concerned a *handful* of books at the margins — never the core Gospels
   and Paul. The picture of wild disagreement is overstated.

Place in this cluster, `related`-linked to `ai-quyet-dinh-sach-nao-thuoc-kinh-thanh`.

## G. NEW CLUSTER (own topic, not this one) — "the unchanging Church that changes"
Owner's idea (tracker line 208). **Do not fold into sola scriptura** — that cluster is about *where
authority comes from*; this is about *whether that authority contradicts itself over time.* Different
objection, enough substance for its own cluster.

**The two keys that make it work:**
1. **Doctrine vs discipline** — most "the Church changed!" examples are *discipline* (fasting rules,
   Latin liturgy, clerical celibacy), which is *meant* to change. Doctrine **develops** (deepens)
   without contradicting. Lead with this; it defuses half the objection.
2. **The owner's seed→tree analogy is Newman's own** — from *An Essay on the Development of Christian
   Doctrine* (1845). Cite it: a named authority beats a bare illustration, and Newman is a convert from
   Anglicanism, which lands especially well with a Protestant reader.

**Likely members:** "did the Church invent doctrines later?" · the Marian dogmas (1854 Immaculate
Conception, 1950 Assumption) — defined late, believed early · "did Vatican II change the faith?" ·
doctrine vs discipline · Newman's development of doctrine. **Verify every date/definition.**
