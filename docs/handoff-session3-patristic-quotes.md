# Hand-off to Session 3 — patristic citations inside the Giải Đáp clusters

From **Session 12** (fact-verification audit), 2026-08-21. Source of record:
`docs/fact-verification-audit.md` — each block below is that report's "Surface B" for the entry.

## What this is

Session 12 audits `content/giao-phu` and `content/cong-dong`. While verifying the Church Fathers it
also checked **every place those Fathers are quoted or relied on inside `content/giai-dap`**, because
that is where their words do argumentative work rather than sit in a biography.

`content/giai-dap` is **Session 3's lane**. Owner's decision: Session 12 verifies and reports,
Session 3 applies. **Nothing below has been applied.** No file under `content/giai-dap` was modified
by this audit.

## Why these were checked ahead of the rest of the audit

The precedent is Giáo Phụ entry 1 (Clement of Rome): the headline quote's key phrase appears in **no**
standard translation, **and the site's apostolic-succession argument hung on that clause.** A wrong
date in a biography is an error; a wrong quote in a Q&A is a broken argument — on a site whose whole
claim is being sourced and checkable, and with all of this live on the public domain now.

## The quotation chain here is longer than anywhere else on the site

Giáo Phụ quotes are bilingual against a named translation. The Q&A quotes are a **Vietnamese
rendering of an English translation of a Latin or Greek original**, often with no edition named.
Verifiers checked the Vietnamese against the underlying text rather than against an English version
of it, and also checked the chapter reference, the `sources:` frontmatter URL, the `refs_ccc`
paragraphs at vatican.va, and the framing claims around each quote.

## Verdict key

`confirmed` — checked and correct; **do not "fix" it.** · `corrected` — definite error, exact
Vietnamese replacement proposed · `contested` — sources genuinely disagree, hedged wording proposed ·
`unverifiable` — could not confirm; flag with `[cần kiểm chứng]` or drop the claim.

## Read this first — the two findings that change an argument

1. **The canon Q&A (`ai-quyet-dinh-sach-nao-thuoc-kinh-thanh`) cites a source that contradicts it.**
   The New Testament half is clean: Athanasius's Festal Letter 39, AD 367, exactly 27 books and no
   others. But the transition "Với Cựu Ước cũng vậy" carries Athanasius across to the Old Testament
   and the deuterocanon — where **the same letter says the opposite.** It lists 22 OT books,
   **excludes Esther from the canon**, and places Wisdom, Sirach, Esther, Judith and Tobit outside it
   as "appointed by the Fathers to be read by those who newly join us." The Q&A's **own cited source**
   (newadvent 03267a) shows this. A Protestant reader who follows the site's footnote finds Athanasius
   arguing their case. The argument is *stronger* told straight — Hippo 393 and Carthage 397 are where
   those books are listed flatly as Scripture.

2. **The infant-baptism Q&A splices two works into one unattributed sentence** — the same failure
   shape found in Giáo Phụ entry 2, where two different letters of Ignatius sat inside one set of
   quotation marks. See the Origen block.

**Good news worth recording:** the capstone quotation of the Duy Kinh Thánh cluster — the site's
newest and most contested argument — **is clean.** Collated word by word against the ANF text of
*De Praescriptione* ch. 19 at the exact cited URL: the ellipsis elides one real sentence, nothing
unbracketed was added, no splice, `[Với bọn lạc giáo]` is genuinely absent from the source and
correctly supplies the referent, the URL is the right work, and CCC §113 and §83 both say what the
Q&A says they say.
